## Why this file exists

A common wish for an open-source project is "anyone can use it, but nobody can
take my work, slap their brand on it, and sell it." The first half is what a
free software licence is *for*. The second half is something a licence
fundamentally cannot do: the Open Source Definition (§6, "No Discrimination
Against Fields of Endeavor") forbids restricting how people use the software,
and the GPL specifically permits charging money for copies. A licence that
banned commercial use would not be open source at all.

**Trademark is the mechanism that actually does the job**, and it is the same
one Mozilla, Signal, WordPress and Linux itself rely on. Anyone may take this
code, change it, and sell it — that freedom is the point. What they may not do
is call the result *Scrozz*, because then users can no longer tell which one
came from us. Firefox → Iceweasel is the canonical precedent.

## What you may do, without asking

- **Use** Scrozz for anything, including commercially, privately or in an
  organisation.
- **Study, modify and redistribute** the source under the terms of the GPL.
- **Build it yourself**, for yourself or for machines you administer.
- **Refer to Scrozz by name** — accurately, in prose. Reviews, comparisons,
  tutorials, blog posts, packaging metadata, "works with Scrozz", "a plugin for
  Scrozz", "forked from Scrozz". This is nominative use and it is always
  permitted; you never need our permission to describe what something is.
- **Say your modified version is "based on Scrozz"** or "a fork of Scrozz",
  provided the *name of your thing* is not Scrozz.
- **Redistribute unmodified official builds**, keeping the name and branding
  intact.

## What requires written permission

- Using **"Scrozz"**, or any name confusingly similar to it, as the name of
  your software, product, service, company or domain.
- Using the **Scrozz logo or visual identity** as the identity of your project
  or product.
- Publishing to **any app store, package repository or distribution channel**
  under the Scrozz name — this includes the Mac App Store, Microsoft Store,
  Flathub, Snap Store, Homebrew, AUR, and OS distribution packages.
- Any use that **implies endorsement, affiliation or official status** that
  does not exist.
- Merchandise.

## If you distribute a modified version

Rename it. That is the whole requirement, and it is not adversarial — it exists
so that a bug in your build is not reported against ours, and so a user
downloading "Scrozz" gets the thing we published.

Concretely: change the product name, the application name, the bundle
identifier, and the logo. You are welcome and encouraged to state prominently
that your project is derived from Scrozz, and to link back here.
